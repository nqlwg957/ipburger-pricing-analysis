## What IPBurger actually is

IPBurger has been selling proxies since 2007 out of the US, which makes it older than most of the brands currently bidding against it on the same keywords. That age shows in what the company chose to build: the product line is weighted toward addresses that belong to one customer and stay that way. Static ISP residential proxies, "fresh" dedicated IPs that marketing says have been rested for months before delivery, private dedicated IPs, and dedicated datacenter IPs.

Rotating residential is there too, with a pool advertised at 100M+ IPs across 193–195 countries, sticky sessions capped at 30 minutes, unlimited concurrent sessions, and country, city and ASN targeting. There are Chrome and Firefox extensions, a dashboard with sub-user seats (2 on the starter tier, up to 6 on the higher ones), and a separate VPN product. Payments run through cards, PayPal and crypto.

The static side is narrower than the rotating side, and this matters more than the price tables suggest: ISP addresses are available in six countries (US, UK, Canada, Germany, France, Australia), dedicated datacenter in a similarly short list. If your work involves a market outside those, the rotating pool is your only option there.

## Three things the price grid doesn't tell you

**There is no free trial and no general money-back window.** The refund rules are product-specific and tight:

> Residential and mobile: 3 days from payment, and only if usage stayed under 0.5 GB. ISP proxies: 5 days, under 1 GB. Fresh and private dedicated IPs: 5 days with no usage limit. VPN: 30 days, no limit.

That first order is therefore your test run, priced at full rate. Five dedicated datacenter IPs at $33 or a single ISP address at $29.95 are the cheapest honest ways to sample the network before you move a portfolio of accounts onto it.

**Unused bandwidth doesn't roll over.** IPBurger's own FAQ states clearly that traffic is allocated per billing cycle and resets each month. Leftover gigabytes are gone. If your workload is spiky — a big scrape one month, near silence the next — you're paying for capacity you don't consume.

**The vendor's headline numbers are vendor numbers.** IPBurger publishes figures like a 99.95% success rate across roughly 30M daily requests and sub-second average residential response times. Those are marketing claims, not lab results. The Chrome extension listing shows 4.49 stars from 51 ratings with about 10,000 users, and the recurring complaint in those reviews isn't performance, it's price — plus occasional short disconnects that reconnect on their own. Trustpilot reputation signals are genuinely mixed: IPBurger points to an "Excellent" standing with 400+ reviews, while at least one independent directory reports a far lower average from the same platform. Treat the score as disputed, and treat the product quality question separately from the price question.

## Where IPBurger is the right purchase

If your job is keeping logins alive, the premium is easier to defend. A static ISP address in the US, UK, Germany, France, Canada or Australia that no stranger shares with you is a different product from a rotating pool, and marketplace sellers, agencies running client accounts and ad-verification teams pay for exactly that. The "fresh" dedicated IPs — private addresses with no usage history — target the account-creation case specifically, and the private dedicated line starts at $4.58 per month per IP, which is genuinely cheap for what it is.

Payment flexibility helps those buyers too. Cards for accounting, PayPal for buyer protection, crypto for anyone who'd rather proxy spending didn't show up on a statement.

## Where the arithmetic stops working

The moment your task is measured in gigabytes instead of IPs, IPBurger's minimum per-GB rate becomes the whole story. Rotating residential bottoms out near $7/GB at the 100–150GB step and then stops: there is no cheaper tier above it, so 300GB and 1,000GB cost the same per gigabyte as 100GB. Larger commitments buy nothing.

Independent directory comparisons put that floor far above the market. On 100GB of rotating residential, one comparison lists IPBurger at $700 against roughly $220 for Proxy Seller and around $150 for Novada. On ISP addresses, twenty of them run $599 at IPBurger versus about $39.80 at Proxy-Cheap and $31.60 at BirdProxies in the same table. Those numbers come from third-party directories rather than controlled tests, so treat the exact figures as directional — but the direction matches IPBurger's own published grid, which you can check yourself without trusting anybody's ranking.

Run the realistic budgets. Thirty gigabytes a month, which is a modest price-monitoring or ad-verification workload, lands in the 25–38GB step at $7.78/GB: about $233. A hundred gigabytes for competitor scraping lands at $700.

## FAQ

**Is IPBurger legitimate?**
It has sold proxies under its own brand since 2007 from the US, accepts cards, PayPal and crypto, and lists a Trustpilot presence with 400+ reviews. It's an established operator, not a fly-by-night shop. Its reputation scores are disputed between its own marketing and third-party directories, so judge it on your own test order rather than either claim.

**Does IPBurger offer a free trial?**
No. Refunds exist but are narrow: 3 days for residential and mobile if usage stayed under 0.5GB, 5 days for ISP proxies under 1GB, 5 days for fresh and private dedicated IPs with no usage limit, and 30 days for the VPN.

**Is IPBurger a good choice for large-scale web scraping?**
Not on price. Residential traffic never drops below about $7/GB and there's no better tier above 100–150GB. Pay-as-you-go providers charging near $1/GB, with traffic that doesn't expire, cost a fraction of that on the same jobs.

**Does DataImpulse sell static ISP proxies?**
No. Its lineup is rotating residential, mobile, datacenter and premium residential traffic. If a project needs a fixed residential address that stays with one user, DataImpulse isn't a substitute for IPBurger's ISP product.

**What's the real minimum spend at DataImpulse?**
$5 for the intro package. Multiple reviewers report that later top-ups require at least $50, so plan your first purchase with that in mind — the traffic doesn't expire, so the balance isn't lost, but it does sit there.

**Can I mix the two?**
That's what a lot of teams actually do. Keep a handful of ISP or dedicated addresses at IPBurger for logins and account work, and route the bulk of your scraping and monitoring traffic through a per-GB provider. You pay the premium only where it buys something.

## The verdict

An "ipburger review" that only says "premium provider, premium price" isn't useful.
